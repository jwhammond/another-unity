Horst III is the smallest mission: four rooms with art, the first of them a cave-in that blocks the way in. It is a good place to see the whole method on a small scale.

## The look

Each plate is the original background with its dithering removed, enlarged four times and then repainted in a modern style. The prompt asks for the same room, not a new one: same walls, same lights, same doorways, only richer surfaces. The result is checked against the original and must line up within 2 px, so the objects a player clicks stay where the game expects them.

All four rooms passed that check as repainted plates. None of them is upscale-only.

## Not a room

The original's list has a fifth entry for this world. It is not a room at all, only a list of the objects that trigger advice and hold lines of dialogue, so there is nothing to draw and it is not shown here.

## What animates

The world has no characters of its own; the away team does the walking. Three props move:

- The rubble pile in the first room. It is part of the background in the original, so the repainted plate did not contain it at first. It is now its own layer, and when the phaser clears it the layer plays the fall.
- A large glow in the third room that loops through a few pictures.
- An isolinear rod in the fourth room, which is off until a script turns it on, plays a short run, then goes off again.

The rubble's fall is the heaviest layer of the world, so frames are streamed in with the room and released when the player leaves.

## The cards

There are no characters here, so the sheet shows the four props that have cards.
